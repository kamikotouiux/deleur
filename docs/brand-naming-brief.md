# Brand and naming brief

**For Val**

This is a brief for creating the identity and a compact brandbook. The public name is not selected. Studio Z is an internal working label. The website content is supplied separately for Daniel; the two documents use the same positioning, founder model and portfolio decision.

## 1. What we are building

An independent design agency led by Val Anpilogov and Daniel Kurilenko. One team works across brand, campaigns, websites and digital products, with a unified portfolio and direct creative leadership.

The acquisition program will test referrals, partnerships, direct outreach, social content, public concepts and useful products. The brand must stay recognizable and credible as these entrances change. The visitor should encounter a coherent studio, rather than the internal story of a lead-generation experiment.

**Commercial objective:** help the right buyer recognize the studio's level, understand what it can take responsibility for, and start a useful project conversation. The identity should make the work more convincing and easier to evaluate.

**Central promise:** strong creative direction carried into coherent execution across the touchpoints the project needs.

**Category:** Independent design agency.

**Capability descriptor:** Brand, web and product design.

**Audience-facing situations:** launching, repositioning, rebuilding a website, developing a digital product, or adding a trusted design team to a larger client engagement.

## 2. Audience and the decisions they need to make

| Buyer | What brings them here | What the brand and site must communicate |
|---|---|---|
| Founder | A launch, a new audience, a product or website that needs stronger design | Clear scope, strong judgment, relevant work, direct responsibility |
| Marketing / brand lead | Rebrand, campaign or inconsistent touchpoints | A coherent creative system and ability to carry the idea into assets |
| Product lead | An important workflow, new product experience or design-system need | Structured thinking, legible product work and a defined production boundary |
| Agency / consultant | A client brief needing complementary expertise or capacity | Reliable collaboration, clear handoff, project leadership and respect for the relationship |
| Viewer from a concept, video or tool | Interest in a specific idea or useful resource | Deliver the promised material, then explain the related professional service |

The initial masterbrand is broad enough for these groups. Campaign messages and outreach can speak to a narrower situation. Do not force every visitor into the same industry or assume every content viewer is ready to commission a project.

## 3. Positioning and proof

**Internal positioning statement:**

For founders, marketing and product teams, and agency partners who need a strong design outcome, the studio connects creative direction with brand, web and product execution. Two equal co-founders lead one agency team, with one accountable project lead and a scope shaped around the brief.

**Reasons to believe:**

- Val's campaign, brand-system and creative-direction work.
- Daniel's product-interface and website work.
- The agency team across design, development and creative specialties.
- A portfolio showing the relationship between idea, system and application.
- A clear engagement model, review process and handover.

**Unified portfolio decision:** both bodies of work belong in the new studio's Selected Work. This is an explicit founder instruction. Do not separate them into an apologetic “previous work” collection. Explain actual contributions through case-level roles and credits. Project screenshots demonstrate the artifacts shown; commercial outcomes or client quotes need their own evidence.

**What the brand should leave people believing:**

“They have a point of view. They can handle the brief. I know who will lead it. I can understand how to start.”

## 4. Founder and team architecture

**Public title for both:** Co-founder & Creative Director.

**Focus labels:**

- Val — Brand, campaigns and creative direction.
- Daniel — Product, web and digital experiences.

Equal ownership is reflected in equal visual treatment: same portrait scale, title size, biography length and prominence. The focus labels explain complementary experience; they do not create a senior/junior relationship.

**Team statement:**

“Our designers, developers and creative specialists work together around the brief. One co-founder leads each project and stays involved through delivery.”

This recognizes the real delivery organization without inventing a headcount, employment structure or office network. Show actual collaborators where useful. A simple diagram of disciplines can communicate breadth before a full team directory is ready. Confirm the specific project team in proposals rather than implying both founders and every specialist work on every brief.

## 5. Brand character

| Attribute | What it should look and sound like |
|---|---|
| Clear | A new visitor understands the category, services and next step |
| Distinctive | A recognizable typographic or compositional behavior, beyond a generic template |
| Considered | Strong decisions, selective work, precise detail and coherent reasoning |
| Capable | Project context, practical information and well-presented execution |
| Direct | Ordinary words, visible people, clear responsibility and usable contact |

The work can be bold. The interface should remain calm enough to evaluate the studio, read a case and make contact. Commercial usefulness and expressive art direction can reinforce each other.

## 6. Three creative territories

### A. Deliberate character

**Idea:** thoughtful decisions and distinctive craft presented with commercial composure.

**Visual behavior:** precise editorial grid; purposeful spacing; an expressive but readable type pairing; strong light/dark structure; one memorable accent. Project work carries rich color. The studio identity makes the selection and hierarchy feel intentional.

**Voice:** observant, concise, specific. Explain a choice, its consequence and the work it produces.

**Good applications:** a case study that feels editorial; a proposal that is easy to scan; a quiet but recognizable social frame.

**Risk:** becoming tasteful and anonymous. Require one ownable behavior beyond beige backgrounds and a large serif headline.

### B. Productive tension

**Idea:** two independent creative minds, backed by delivery discipline.

**Visual behavior:** controlled contrast in scale, typography, alignment or motion. A structured information layer supports expressive project presentation. One recognizable transformation or juxtaposition can carry the identity across formats.

**Voice:** assured and willing to take a position, with concrete language about the work. Avoid taunting clients or adopting rebellion as a substitute for a useful offer.

**Good applications:** a wordmark with a distinctive relationship between parts; case covers with a recognizable compositional rule; short motion that reveals an idea.

**Risk:** reading as an art project. Scope, team and contact need a stable, practical treatment.

### C. Connected work

**Idea:** brand, website, campaign and product reinforce one another.

**Visual behavior:** a modular system that adapts to the material; repeated structures across brand artifacts, page layouts and product states; transitions showing how an idea becomes a working application.

**Voice:** active and practical. Name the touchpoint, the decision and what happens next.

**Good applications:** project sequences; a case cover evolving into a website view; consistent proposal, web and social layouts.

**Risk:** generic “full-service” language. Demonstrate the connections with actual work and clear responsibilities.

**Recommended starting direction:** B, with a disciplined information layer. It gives the new studio enough character for creative content while supporting confident business communication. A and C remain useful alternatives if the founders prefer a quieter identity. Choose one system before developing visual applications; do not combine three unrelated moodboards into a single identity.

**Application test:** the distinctive behavior must work in a homepage opening, a case cover and a small social preview. Body text, navigation and form behavior remain stable across all three. If the behavior only works in a full-screen poster, it is not yet a useful studio identity system.

## 7. Naming brief

**The name should:**

- Be understandable and pronounceable in an English introduction.
- Remain useful across brand, web, product and campaign work.
- Sound credible in a proposal, project credit and referral.
- Be memorable enough for social content and a spoken recommendation.
- Allow a distinctive wordmark without depending on a complex symbol.
- Feel separate from Design Punks and Sixcat.

The name does not need to explain every service. A clear descriptor can do that work. Avoid defining the business by low price, a current AI tool, a specific platform, a geography or the fact there are two founders.

### Recommended discussion shortlist

| Name | Strategic fit | Main concern |
|---|---|---|
| **Curious Standard** | Exploration held to a clear standard; memorable contrast; broad enough for creative and product work | “Standard” may sound conventional; the phrase is already used for a notebook product, so distinctiveness needs further review |
| **Purpose in Form** | Connects intention with tangible design; calm and commercially legible | Longer and phrase-like; an existing furniture-design exhibition uses this exact title |
| **Force of Detail** | A strong craft proposition with an assertive voice | Could imply a narrow focus on polishing rather than larger direction |

**My first creative choice to explore:** Curious Standard. Test it in a wordmark, a project credit and a founder introduction before committing to it. It is a recommendation for exploration, not a selected or cleared business name.

### Wider longlist

Clear Substance · Form & Consequence · Good Bearings · Particular Matter · Useful Obsession · Tender Force · Open Nerve · Better Tension · Exact Instinct · Motive Assembly · Joined Things · Woven Direction · Common Current · Full Thread · Made in Relation

These are naming proposals, not claims that the phrases are commercially unused. Several are weaker on search distinctiveness, category associations or near-name competition. Do not treat all 18 names as equally screened finalists.

### Preliminary public-name check

On 9 November 2026, limited public exact-phrase searches did not surface a relevant exact studio identity for Curious Standard, Purpose in Form or Force of Detail. They did reveal existing uses: [Curious Book Co](https://www.curiousbookco.com/news/notebooks-for-conferences/) calls one notebook product The Curious Standard, and the [University of Oklahoma](https://www.ou.edu/gibbs/divisions/interior-design/furniture-design-studio/news) reports a furniture-design exhibition titled Purpose in Form. These are not established design-agency conflicts, but they affect search and association. No phrase is represented as unused or available. Woven Direction surfaced in textile language; Motive Assembly has relevant near-name competition from the creative studio [MOTIVE](https://www.bymotive.com/).

Already occupied directions excluded from the lead shortlist include [Odd Standard](https://www.oddstandard.no/design-studio), [Soft Voltage](https://softvoltage.com/) and [WholeSignal](https://www.wholesignal.ca/). Adding “Studio” does not reliably resolve confusion with an occupied core name.

No trademark, company-name, domain, social-handle or linguistic clearance has been completed. No domain has been purchased or represented as available. Before adopting a finalist, check the actual markets, relevant business categories, confusingly similar names, domain and handles. Record findings and the final decision. Content development can continue name-neutral while that choice is made.

### Practical name test

Put each finalist into the following situations:

- “I'm Daniel, co-founder of ___.”
- “We brought in ___ for the launch.”
- “Brand and website by ___.”
- A mobile header, email signature, proposal cover and invoice line.

Ask a few English-speaking target buyers to repeat and spell the name after hearing it once, describe their first category association and explain what they expect the studio to do. This is a qualitative ambiguity check, not a statistically valid popularity contest.

## 8. Verbal identity

**Canonical descriptor:** Independent design agency.

**Capability line:** Brand, web and product design.

**Homepage opening:** Daniel will choose from the four hero options in the current landing brief. No headline has been selected:

- Be the one they remember.
- Make the brand as interesting as the business.
- Let’s make something people give a damn about.
- Your business has moved on. Has your design?

**Short studio description:**

“We bring creative direction and hands-on design together across brands, websites and digital products. Our co-founders lead a focused team shaped around each brief.”

**Founder/team line:** Led by two co-founders. Built around your brief.

**Primary call to action:** Talk about your project.

### Writing rules

Use concrete nouns and verbs: define, design, develop, review, build, hand over. Explain what is included and what depends on the brief. Sound like a capable person speaking to another capable person.

Support statements about scale, quality, speed and results with the actual work or operating process. “One co-founder leads your engagement” is a usable commitment. “World-class results, ten times faster” needs evidence and rarely helps explain the purchase.

Avoid generic superlatives, long manifestos, tool worship and repeated claims about disrupting agencies. Avoid publishing the internal language of growth experiments, reputation isolation or lead acquisition as the studio's origin story.

| Situation | Preferred language |
|---|---|
| Explain breadth | “Brand, web and product design.” |
| Explain leadership | “One co-founder leads your engagement through delivery.” |
| Explain team | “The team and responsibilities are agreed around the brief.” |
| Explain scope | “Your proposal defines the deliverables, reviews and production boundary.” |
| Discuss uncertainty | “Tell us what is changing. We can help define a useful first scope.” |
| Describe AI | “AI-assisted tools can support exploration and production; judgment and review stay with the team.” |

## 9. Identity system to develop

### Wordmark and marks

Develop one primary wordmark, a compact version and monochrome/inverse variants. A symbol is optional; it must add recognition or practical flexibility. Define minimum sizes, clear space, alignment and behavior beside project imagery. Prepare a legible favicon/social avatar.

### Typography

Choose a small type system with a clear hierarchy for headlines, case titles, body text, captions, metadata, form labels and buttons. Two families are a practical ceiling for the first release. Test real English text, long client names, budget ranges and error messages. Supply actual webfont files, weights, fallbacks and licensing information. Prioritize readable body copy and useful mobile line breaks.

### Color

Define text, background, surface, accent, border, link, focus, error and success roles. Keep functional color behavior separate from project colors. Check required text and control contrast in the actual combinations. Do not use color alone to communicate state. A distinctive accent is useful only if it remains usable across light/dark and interactive states.

### Grid and composition

Define a responsive grid, spacing scale, section rhythm and content widths. Test a wide desktop composition, a conventional laptop viewport and a narrow mobile page. Specify how expressive work sits inside readable case information. Large type must wrap intentionally rather than clip or force horizontal scrolling.

### Project imagery

Use actual work as the main proof. Establish rules for covers, device frames, crops, background color, sequences, captions and close-ups. Include enough legible detail to evaluate interface work. Show a system through meaningful applications rather than repeated decorative mockups. Concept work retains a clear status label.

### People and team

Give both founders an equal portrait treatment. Decide whether the studio uses photography, a consistent illustrated portrait system or a deliberately art-directed combination. Every named person must be real. Establish a quieter team treatment that can expand beyond the founders without redesigning the whole site.

### Motion

Choose one or two behaviors that express the brand: transformation, alignment, contrast or continuity. Define their purpose, typical duration/easing approach and reduced-motion alternative. The offer, case navigation and contact must remain useful with motion disabled. A custom cursor or WebGL scene is optional, not a first-release requirement.

### Interface behavior

Show buttons, text links, navigation, service blocks, case cards, FAQ, form fields, errors, success messages and focus states. These are part of the identity as experienced by a buyer. Avoid creating only a striking homepage cover while leaving the inquiry flow visually undefined.

## 10. Deliverables for Daniel

**First-release minimum:** selected identity principle; wordmark; type and functional color roles; responsive homepage opening; one case treatment; contact/form treatment; essential logo/font/color exports. Daniel can start from this compact system. The fuller table below is the expanded handoff, not a requirement to complete every application before building begins. Proposal, partner-page and additional social applications can follow.

| Asset | Minimum useful handoff |
|---|---|
| Logo | SVG primary/compact, monochrome and inverse; PNG exports where needed |
| Favicon/avatar | Recognizable small mark; relevant web/social exports |
| Typography | Families, licensed files/links, weights, hierarchy, mobile behavior and fallbacks |
| Color | Role-based values and tested functional combinations |
| Layout | Grid, spacing, widths, image ratios and responsive examples |
| Case system | Cover, detail sequence, caption/credit treatment and CTA treatment |
| People | Founder portrait treatment and expandable team pattern |
| Components | Header, primary/secondary actions, service and engagement cards, form, FAQ, footer |
| Motion | A concise principle, one demonstrated behavior and reduced-motion state |
| Voice | Descriptor, short description, CTA system and several real examples |
| Applications | Homepage opening, one slider project, contact-section opening, contact section, proposal cover and social preview |

Use editable source files and organized exports. Include semantic role names such as text, background, accent and focus, so Daniel can translate the system into the build. The handoff is ready when real content works across these applications, not when the brandbook reaches a particular page count.

## 11. Compact brandbook structure

1. Studio purpose, category and audiences.
2. Positioning, team model and proof.
3. Name decision and descriptor.
4. Identity concept and wordmark rules.
5. Typography and color roles.
6. Grid, composition and image behavior.
7. Founder/team treatment.
8. Interface and motion behavior.
9. Voice and actual English examples.
10. Website, case, partner, proposal and social applications.
11. Asset index and handoff notes.

Build only as much documentation as is needed to keep these decisions consistent. The longer system can grow after the acquisition experiments produce useful information.

## 12. Sequence for the first release

**9 November:** settle the category, content architecture and first visual territory. Shortlist names and prepare the real cases.

**10 November:** develop the chosen identity through a wordmark, type/color system, hero, case treatment and contact section. Test at mobile size. Package the essential assets and rules for Daniel.

Record two decisions: the chosen visual territory and either the chosen public name or an explicit choice to continue the layout name-neutral temporarily. Final wordmark exports, domain/email and metadata depend on the name decision; content and page composition can proceed in parallel.

**Website build:** Daniel builds the first release from the current landing-page content pack. Review actual pages together for clarity, visual coherence, case legibility and inquiry usability. Add detail to the brandbook where the real build exposes a gap.

These dates reflect the plan in the shared meeting notes. They are a working schedule, not evidence that the identity or website has already been produced.
