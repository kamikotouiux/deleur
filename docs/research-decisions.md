# Final research and editorial decisions

The current production brief is **daniel-landing-brief.pdf**, with a standalone HTML version and editable English source. It describes one landing page with seven sections and eight projects selected by Daniel. Val Anpilogov and Daniel Kurilenko are equal **Co-founder & Creative Director**, leading one agency team. This replaces the earlier extremely short draft and the earlier multi-page proposal.

## What the research supports

There is no demonstrated ideal word count or highest-converting section order for this particular agency. The useful target is a complete, easy-to-scan buying explanation: what can be commissioned, relevant evidence, who is responsible, how the engagement works and how to start. The shared page copy is about 640 words before Daniel adds the eight case names, categories and captions. A complete page will be longer; the examples in the brief guide those descriptions without choosing the projects for him. That is an editorial choice for this material, not a universal conversion benchmark.

The site needs to work for a founder, marketing or brand lead, product lead, or agency partner. Visitors may arrive from a referral, outreach, a creative concept or social content. Some will make a quick credibility check; others will need enough information to share internally before contacting the agency. The broad portfolio can stay broad while each acquisition experiment speaks to a specific buyer and problem.

## Additional pass: character and desire

The current landing brief supplies four hero options, and Daniel will choose the opening. No headline has been selected or established as a conversion winner. The supporting text names brands, campaigns, websites and digital products. Launch, rebrand and product-change situations make the invitation concrete. Direct founder leadership supplies a reason to trust the work.

The four options are original draft copy for this agency, not quoted lines from reference studios. They offer different degrees of aspiration, personality and buyer-specific tension. Daniel's choice will work with the supporting text; the headline is not intended to explain all the services on its own.

### Additional primary evidence

Source coverage: the NN/g page was read in full; the journal papers were reviewed through primary abstracts and indexed publisher/full-text excerpts, not a complete reading of both papers.

- [NN/g: The Impact of Tone of Voice on Users' Brand Perception](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tone-voice-users/), reviewed in 2024, compares paired fictional website texts through qualitative work and a 100-person US survey. Tone affected impressions of friendliness, trust and willingness to recommend. A playful version could feel friendlier while reducing trust. Application: conversational confidence with practical facts kept plain. These are perceptions of particular samples, not observed purchases of agency services.
- [Shulman, Markowitz and Rogers: Reading dies in complexity](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/publications/reading-dies-complexity-online-news-consumers-prefer-simple-writing), *Science Advances*, 2024, analyses more than 30,000 headline field tests and includes a mechanism experiment. Simpler writing attracted and held general readers' attention; professional writers responded differently. Application: use familiar words and an immediate idea. News attention does not establish qualified agency enquiries or prove that one particular word causes a sale.
- [Packard and Berger: How Concrete Language Shapes Customer Satisfaction](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article/47/5/787/5873524), *Journal of Consumer Research*, 2020/2021, combines real service interactions with experiments. Concrete, relevant language could signal that the employee understood the customer's needs. Application: launches, packaging, websites and workflows provide useful specifics. The studied retail/service conversations differ from agency homepages; the application is a writing hypothesis.

### What changed across the page

The opening now expresses a desire instead of a workflow principle. Services begin with a purpose the buyer can recognise, followed by actual outputs. The process describes concrete collaboration in shorter sentences. The founders remain visible and accountable. Practical questions still explain budget, timing, agency partnerships and partial scope. The contact invitation uses real starting points: a launch date, a rough idea or a screen that needs work. The primary action is **Talk about your project**; the form action remains **Send project details**.

The voice is conversational, confident and lightly playful. The claims stay tied to the work. No sales, attention, popularity or financial outcome is guaranteed. No unadopted price or fictional delivery time has been introduced. The point is to make the offer more desirable while preserving enough substance to choose it.

## Buying information and reading evidence

- [NN/g: B2B Usability](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/b2b-usability/) describes research-heavy business buying with several criteria and sometimes several participants. This historical research supports useful scope and delivery information; it does not prescribe a modern agency funnel.
- [NN/g: Formatting Long-Form Content](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/formatting-long-form-content/) supports editing, useful grouping and visible structure when information is substantial. Its content-length categories are not an ideal word count for this site.
- [NN/g: Layer-Cake Scanning](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/layer-cake-pattern-scanning/) shows the value of descriptive, noticeable headings. The brief therefore uses actual section headings and coherent short paragraphs, without repeated handoff labels.
- [NN/g: About Us Information](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/about-us-information-on-websites/) identifies problems with vague company explanations, artificial imagery and corporate language. It supports clear category information, real people and understandable contact routes.
- [NN/g: Prices for Common Scenarios](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/show-prices-for-common-scenarios/) supports helping buyers estimate commitment when custom pricing is complicated. It does not establish this agency's fees or prove that hiding prices creates a premium impression.
- [GOV.UK: Writing for User Interfaces](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/design/writing-for-user-interfaces) supports short, direct sentences and meaningful action labels. Its government tone rules are not imposed on the agency's personality.
- [GOV.UK: Question Pages](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/question-pages/) supports asking purposeful questions and identifying optional information. The contact form asks for a name, email and project description; company, budget and timing remain optional. This is a proportionate proposal, not a proven field-count formula.
- [Hinge: How Professional Services Buyers Check You Out](https://hingemarketing.com/blog/story/new-research-study-how-professional-services-buyers-check-you-out) distinguishes finding a provider from evaluating it through websites and other channels. Its self-reported 2014 survey is useful background, not a current design-agency conversion estimate.

These sources support information needs and writing decisions. None verifies the sales performance of the proposed page.

## Why seven sections

| Section | The question it answers |
|---|---|
| Opening | What is this agency, what does it make and why is its approach interesting? |
| Selected work | Has it made work relevant to my problem, and what was the actual scope? |
| What we can make | Which part of a brand, website or digital product can it take on? |
| How we get there | How can we start, what gets agreed, how are decisions made and what is handed over? |
| You'll know who's making it | Who leads the work, who is on the team and who is responsible? |
| Practical questions | How are cost and timing established? Can the agency collaborate or take a partial scope? |
| Contact | What should I send, what happens next and can I use email instead? |

Three engagement formats remain: a focused sprint, a defined project and ongoing work. They are explained inside the working process, rather than becoming a second catalogue beside the services. A sprint explores a direction or prototype; complete production is a separate scope. Website development can be included; product design does not automatically promise application engineering.

The current creative through-line is **work with a character of its own**. Campaigns, identities, websites and interfaces can all substantiate it. Creative continuity remains part of the delivery explanation; the opening now leads with appetite and presence. The work slider should demonstrate range and distinct decisions, with useful captions selected and written by Daniel.

## Proof, pricing and release decisions

Daniel selects four cases from [Val's work](https://anpilogov.framer.website/) and four from [his own portfolio](https://danielkurilenko.com/), including their order. They are presented as one agency portfolio, as requested. The brief gives a reusable format: actual name, one or two service categories, useful media and a short sentence about the design task. The example descriptions are writing illustrations, not a selected shortlist. Final captions should describe the actual work and scope. No new commercial outcomes, testimonials, awards or delivery metrics have been added. Some source portfolios contain numerical claims; those have not been adopted without supporting measurement information.

The founders have not adopted numerical packages. The page therefore explains fee drivers and an agreed proposal without inventing a starting price. When a repeatable scope is ready to sell at a fee the team will honour, add a representative example or range. Quote-led pricing is a first-release decision, not a claim that secrecy makes an agency expensive.

Daniel retains design, UX/UI and interaction decisions. The brief supplies content relationships and CTA destinations, not a mandated visual layout. The first build remains a single page using existing case media. It needs no additional case pages, resource library, new video production or custom availability system. The actual build time depends on assets and the implementation Daniel chooses; 1–2 days is the requested production scope, not a delivery guarantee to clients.
